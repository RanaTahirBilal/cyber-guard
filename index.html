06 The real world

The attacks in the news are made of the things in stage four.

Anil takes a real attack apart on the channel and shows which piece of it is which. Once you can name the parts, the news stops being frightening and starts being readable.

The email that is hunting you Anatomy of a phishing attack
The part you see An email from a service you actually use, about a problem you would care about.
What it really is

Reconnaissance. The attacker knew which service to imitate because that was findable before a single email was sent. Stage 03.

The part you see A link that looks almost exactly right.
What it really is

A domain built to survive a two-second glance and fail a five-second one. Reading a URL properly is a skill, and it is taught. Stage 04.

The part you see A login page that behaves normally.
What it really is

A credential harvester. It never logs you in — it forwards you to the real site afterwards so nothing feels wrong. Stage 05.

The part you see Nothing. For weeks.
What it really is

The reason phishing works. The gap between the mistake and the consequence is long enough that most people never connect the two. Stage 06.

Every stage referenced above is a stage on the path in the section before this one. That is the argument of this entire site: the thing on the news and the thing in the curriculum are the same thing, seen from two ends.

01 · Where he started

Nobody can copy where you started.

Anil is from a small village in Lumbini Province, Nepal. Middle class, ordinary, and nothing in the surroundings that pointed at a career in cybersecurity.

No family in the field. No school that taught it. Nobody to ask. He found it himself, starting with a general curiosity about computers and following it until it turned into something specific enough to be called a subject.

That matters more than it sounds. Most people who teach this started somewhere that made it easy. He did not — which is exactly why he can explain it to someone starting from nothing.

anil@lab — the first thing that worked
# no course, no teacher, no order to any of it
$ whoami
anil
$ cat /etc/os-release | head -1
PRETTY_NAME="Kali GNU/Linux"
$ ping -c 1 10.10.14.7
64 bytes from 10.10.14.7: icmp_seq=1 ttl=64 time=0.42 ms
# one reply from a machine he built himself.
# that was the whole beginning.

02 · The year nobody understood

A year that looked like nothing was happening.

Hours on labs, tools and concepts. Repeating whatever did not make sense the first time, and then repeating it again.

Free material on the internet is enormous and completely unordered. A video here, a blog post there, three tools that all claim to do the same thing, and nothing telling you what order any of it goes in. He worked through it alone.

From the outside it looked like a year being wasted. People said so.

The year people thought I was wasting turned out to be the most valuable investment of my life.

05 · The teaching

He explains it the way he needed it explained.

In Hindi, from the beginning, without assuming you already know the words.

Most cybersecurity teaching does one of two things. It shows you a tool and calls that a lesson, or it assumes a background you do not have and moves on before you have caught up. Both leave you able to repeat a command and unable to explain what it did.

His goes the other way. What the thing is, why it works, then the tool, then you do it yourself in a lab you are allowed to break. It is slower, and it is the reason people finish.

the difference
□ most tutorials
$ sqlmap -u http://target/item?id=1 --dbs
[*] available databases [2]:
[*] information_schema
[*] shopdb
# you can now repeat this. you cannot explain it.

□ this course
# 1. what a database query actually is
# 2. why unchecked input reaches it
# 3. what that lets an attacker read
# 4. then the tool
# 5. then you do it, on your own lab

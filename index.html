08 What he does

Two audiences. One problem.

People assume these are separate jobs. They are the same job pointed at two kinds of listener.

For people entering the field

Learn cybersecurity

  • Networking, DNS, HTTP

  • Web security and authentication

For everybody else

What the attack in the news actually was, and what it means for an ordinary phone.

  • Phishing and email fraud
  • Scams and social engineering
  • Spyware and device threats
  • How home networks get reached
  • What protects you and what does not

One mission, either way: make cybersecurity understandable.

09 The teacher

He explains it the way he needed it explained.

In Hindi, from the beginning, without assuming you already know the words.

Most cybersecurity teaching shows you a tool and calls that a lesson. You end up able to repeat a command and unable to say what it did. His goes the other way round, and it is slower for exactly that reason.

FirstWhat it is

The thing itself, in words you already have.

ThenWhy it works

The mechanism underneath. The part almost everybody skips.

ThenThe tool

Which now makes sense, because you know what it is doing for you.

ThenYou try it

On a machine you own and are allowed to break.

ResultYou can explain it

Not repeat it. Explain it.

10 What he believes

Three things his own experience taught him.

Information is everywhere. Direction isn't.

He never lacked material. He lacked anybody to say which thing came first — and that, not the material, is what cost him years.

Cybersecurity should be understandable before it becomes complicated.

The jargon arrives long before the concepts do. He teaches in the opposite order, which is why he teaches in Hindi from the first minute.

Being a few steps ahead beats being an expert.

He still remembers which part was confusing, because it confused him recently. That memory is the thing an expert has usually lost.
